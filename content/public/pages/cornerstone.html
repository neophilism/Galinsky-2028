WHY IT MATTERS

A public decision should not leave a private debt unpaid.

An industry can change for many reasons. The Cornerstone addresses a narrower problem: identifiable workers whose lawful livelihoods are deliberately destroyed or materially contracted by government action.

The remedy would be a productive replacement opportunity owned and governed by the affected workers. It is not a promise to restore every old job or a guarantee that a new business will succeed.

THE MECHANISM

What would change.

01

Establish the injury.

Use evidence and fixed certification rules to connect a government action to the workers and livelihoods actually harmed.

02

Let workers organize.

Eligible workers form voluntary associations, with equal founding ownership and one-member-one-vote control.

03

Negotiate what comes next.

Associations negotiate new commercial opportunities with participating states. Training and startup support help turn an opportunity into an operating enterprise.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

Details matter.

Does every displaced worker qualify?

No. The proposal uses a defined government-action trigger, evidence of causation, and certification. A research census entry is not an eligibility determination.

Would outside funding take control away from workers?

Worker control is central. The design separates an economic return from governing power; a public surplus share does not itself buy a vote, board seat, or management authority.

Is a successful business guaranteed?

No. The remedy is an enforceable opportunity, not a guaranteed profit. Commercial feasibility, training, transparent rules, and independent safeguards still matter.

THE SAFEGUARDS

How we keep
the principle intact.

  • Traceable harm, not every market change
  • Voluntary worker participation
  • Worker voting control
  • No forced relocation, training, or personal debt
  • Ordinary safety and public-interest rules
