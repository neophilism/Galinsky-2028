01 / THE MISSION

Make human civilization
permanent.

Humanity's future should not depend forever on the survival of one planet.

Eternal Liberty would make permanent expansion beyond Earth a national strategic ambition: develop the means to live, work, build, research, and ultimately sustain communities beyond our home world.

That requires durable infrastructure, responsible resource use, closed-loop life-support and food systems, autonomous industry, scientific cooperation, and a long view of the generations who will live with today's decisions.

The farther horizon is vivigenesis: carrying living systems into environments where life has never existed, with scientific care and respect for planetary-protection obligations. It is an aspiration, not a claim that such technology is ready.

02 / FREEDOM TRAVELS WITH US

Carry the rights.
Not a claim to the land.

Where American authority goes, American accountability must follow.

The program would condition U.S. sponsorship and support on enforceable civil-rights safeguards for people under the authority of American-backed entities. Protections must be meaningful in day-to-day operations, not aspirational language in a charter.

  • Enforceable protections. Establish clear rights, due process, independent investigation, and effective remedies.
  • Access to justice. Fund legal assistance and channels for reporting misconduct and pursuing accountability.
  • Precise jurisdiction. Apply U.S. obligations to U.S.-backed entities and people acting under their authority, with rules compatible with applicable international law.
  • No celestial annexation. Protect people without asserting U.S. ownership or sovereignty over the Moon, Mars, or other celestial territory.

This is a proposed statutory and contractual framework. Its precise jurisdiction, enforcement mechanisms, and treaty interfaces would require legislative drafting.

03 / SPACE PROGRAM INCOME SHARE AGREEMENTS

Public capital.
Private initiative.
Success pays forward.

SPISA would invest in frontier activities on terms designed to help the program replenish itself when supported enterprises become successful.

01 — FINANCE

Back the frontier

Provide interest-free, mission-aligned financing to eligible high-risk ventures and enabling infrastructure.

02 — EARN

Repayment follows success

Repayment begins only once the supported activity earns the qualifying income defined in its agreement.

03 — REINVEST

Fund what comes next

Set the repayment multiple actuarially rather than promising one fixed return; use realized returns to support further ventures.

SPISA is a proposed financing mechanism. Terms, risk controls, eligibility, accounting and legislative authority would need to be specified publicly before implementation. It is neither a guaranteed return nor an existing program.

04 / SCIENCE & STEWARDSHIP

Explore boldly.
Preserve wisely.

Some of the universe's most important scientific places cannot be replaced once disturbed.

The Lunar Far Side Reserve is a separate, complementary policy: preserve a substantial radio-quiet region of the Moon's far side for astronomy and scientific inquiry, using international cooperation and carefully bounded operations.

Eternal Liberty governs U.S.-backed human expansion and the rights of people under American authority. The lunar-reserve initiative governs preservation of a uniquely valuable scientific environment. They work together without confusing science stewardship with sovereignty claims.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

Ambition needs accountability.

Does this proposal claim American sovereignty over the Moon or Mars?

No. The aim is to attach enforceable protections to American-backed operations and people under their authority, not to claim ownership of celestial territory.

How is SPISA different from an ordinary government loan?

Instead of an interest-bearing schedule regardless of outcomes, the proposed agreement would begin repayment only once defined earnings occur, using an actuarially set return multiple. Specific terms and taxpayer safeguards would require legislation.

Does this mean forcing other nations to follow American law?

No. The proposal would define obligations for U.S.-backed entities and those exercising their authority while respecting international commitments and other nations' jurisdiction.

Is vivigenesis possible today?

It is a long-term scientific ambition, not a claim of present capability. Any future effort must address planetary protection, ecological safety, and scientific uncertainty.
