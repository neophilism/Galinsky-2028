WHY IT MATTERS

Freedom is not a reward for agreeing with the government.

Speech and democratic participation give the American people the power to correct their government. Those protections must hold when an opinion is unpopular, a challenger is inconvenient, or the people in power would rather not be questioned.

Kirk–Evers brings a single principle to that problem: no government should use coercion, retaliation, or manipulation to silence lawful political participation.

THE MECHANISM

What would change.

01

Defend lawful expression.

Restrict government-coerced censorship and retaliation against lawful political speech.

02

Protect democratic participation.

Address intimidation, deceptive interference, and abuses that undermine a person’s ability to participate freely.

03

Make rights enforceable.

Provide legal paths to challenge violations, with transparent oversight and safeguards against abuse.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

Details matter.

Does this protect people who disagree with Phillip?

Yes. The purpose is a consistent rule that protects lawful expression and participation, including the views of political opponents.

Is this already federal law?

No. It is a campaign legislative proposal. The publication version of the bill, its summary, and supporting documents will be available here once approved.

Why Kirk–Evers?

The name honors Charlie Kirk and Medgar Evers across different eras and beliefs. The principle is that no one should face political violence for advocating their convictions.
