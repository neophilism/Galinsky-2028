# National CO₂ Pipeline Policy Action Kit

*Turn captured industrial carbon into products through a voluntary transport network.*

Explain the Restore Natural Abundance proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The proposal would begin a dedicated CO₂ network in high-concentration industrial corridors and deliver captured gas to controlled greenhouses, algae systems, fuels, materials, and durable storage—with routes only across land from willing sellers.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Industrial facilities emit concentrated carbon dioxide that can sometimes be captured and used as a feedstock, while users are often far from sources. Transport, safety, lifecycle emissions, economics, and markets determine whether reuse is beneficial.

1. Map suitable high-purity sources and verifiable users before choosing routes.
2. Acquire every route voluntarily without eminent domain under the platform’s rule.
3. Build to rigorous CO₂-specific engineering, fracture-control, impurity, monitoring, emergency-response, and public-disclosure standards.
4. Deliver to audited greenhouses, algae bioreactors, fuel/material projects, or durable storage where full lifecycle analysis demonstrates value.
5. Scale only when contracts, safety performance, market demand, community consent, water/energy use, and carbon accounting justify expansion.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No eminent domain; willing sellers only
- PHMSA/DOT safety regulation, local emergency planning, plume modeling, monitoring, and incident transparency
- Lifecycle accounting that includes capture energy, transport, leakage, product use, and permanence
- No claim that plants convert CO₂ into ‘clean oxygen’ at a simple one-for-one industrial scale
- No pipeline before real offtake, finance, and community/tribal consultation

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say captured CO₂ is automatically removed permanently; fuel and food uses can return carbon to the atmosphere.
- Do not call CO₂ harmless; concentrated releases can asphyxiate and require specialized emergency planning.
- Do not identify a proposed route or affected property without official study.
- Do not promise profit or national scale before a corridor pilot proves safety and markets.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is using CO₂ the same as storing it? | No. Durability depends on the product and lifecycle. Fuels and food are generally short-cycle uses; mineralization and some materials/storage can be longer-lived. |
| Are CO₂ pipelines safe? | They are regulated infrastructure with specific hazards. The proposal must meet updated federal standards, emergency planning, monitoring, and community review. |
| Why a pipeline? | A shared network can connect clustered sources to verified users, but only where economics, safety, consent, and lifecycle benefits beat alternatives. |
| Would land be taken? | The platform says no eminent domain; routes use land from willing sellers. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Lead with the distinction between utilization and durable storage.
2. Host a corridor-safeguards discussion with residents, tribes, landowners, first responders, pipeline engineers, workers, environmental-justice advocates, and product users.
3. Use DOE and PHMSA sources for current technology and safety law.
4. Never map speculative routes onto private property in campaign material.
