# Three Sisters Rewilding & Indigenous Empowerment Action Kit

*Support Native-led restoration on traditional territories through consent and repayable investment.*

Explain the Restore Natural Abundance proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The act would recognize a leading role for Native nations in restoring traditional territories through cultural burning, forest gardening, and related stewardship, financed in part through income-sharing agreements with participating landowners.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Many landscapes face hazardous fuels, biodiversity loss, eroded soil, and disrupted stewardship. Indigenous fire and land practices were suppressed even where they can improve ecological and cultural outcomes.

1. Begin with government-to-government consultation and free, prior, meaningful participation by the relevant tribal nation—not a generic ‘Indigenous’ contractor.
2. Identify traditional territory, title, treaty, sacred-site, cultural-property, and knowledge-governance issues before project design.
3. Co-design cultural burning, wild tending, forest gardening, Three Sisters planting, and complementary practices for the specific ecosystem.
4. Use a Wild Harvest Income Sharing Agreement for willing private, municipal, or state landowners and separately lawful authorities for federal land.
5. Measure ecological, fire, food, cultural, labor, liability, and financial outcomes; share benefits and protect sensitive knowledge.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Tribal sovereignty and government-to-government consultation
- No extraction or publication of traditional knowledge without permission and benefit sharing
- Tribal lead does not eliminate fire, smoke, safety, species, water, labor, or environmental law
- No single cost number is treated as the universal annual wildfire loss
- Long-term stewardship and liability funding—not one-time projects

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not treat Native nations as one group or claim one nation’s authority in another’s territory.
- Do not copy ceremonies, sacred knowledge, or cultural practices into volunteer events.
- Do not promise access to tribal lands or federal projects.
- Do not state that every cultural burn, planting system, or raised bed fits every ecosystem.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| What does ‘Native-led’ mean? | The relevant nation has a real role in authority, design, knowledge governance, work, and benefits—not ceremonial consultation after decisions are made. |
| Is prescribed or cultural fire risk-free? | No. It requires specific knowledge, planning, weather, smoke management, permits, trained crews, and liability systems. |
| How does WHISA repay? | The platform proposes a fixed share of future harvest income across a portfolio, with part of harvest donated locally. Exact pricing and risk rules require design. |
| Does this override private property? | Participation by private landowners is voluntary; public-land authority and tribal rights require their own law. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Invite the specific local tribal government or authorized program to decide whether and how it wishes to engage; no response is not consent.
2. Center tribal and federal sources on cultural burning and stewardship.
3. Hold a safeguards session about sovereignty, knowledge ownership, smoke, liability, labor, ecosystem fit, and benefit sharing.
4. Volunteer on land only through an authorized stewardship program.
