# Event Host Playbook

*Host a small, safe gathering that produces a real next action.*

Run a house discussion, policy salon, watch party, tabling meetup, or human call-bank party without binding the campaign or mishandling money and data.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- A clear agenda and accessible setting
- No cash collection, unauthorized spending, or hidden recording
- Every attendee chooses one voluntary next step

> **Your 60-minute start:** Invite four people to a forty-five-minute discussion of one policy, using your home or a lawful no-cost space.

## Set up the action

1. Choose a small format and a no-cost location you control or have permission to use.
2. Check occupancy, accessibility, parking/transit, noise, food-allergy, and emergency needs.
3. State whether photos are planned and obtain opt-in consent; create a camera-free area.
4. Do not charge admission, sell items, accept money, or promise reimbursement.
5. Print the agenda and discussion rules; assign timekeeper and safety contact.
6. End with the official signup/donation link and anonymous totals.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** Welcome. I’m hosting as a volunteer, not as an official campaign spokesperson. Tonight we’ll examine one published proposal, hear questions, and each decide whether to take a next step. Disagreement is welcome; personal attacks and recording without consent are not.

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Before we leave, choose one: read the full platform, share one accurate link, join a role, or use the official donation form. You never need to give me your personal information.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| An attendee dominates | Thank them, restate the time limit, and invite someone who has not spoken. |
| A guest starts fundraising | Stop money collection and direct everyone to the official form. |
| A call-bank guest brings automation software | Do not use it; manual human calls only through the approved system. |
| Conflict escalates | Pause, restate conduct rules, and end the event if safety or dignity cannot be restored. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Signing venue/vendor contracts in the campaign’s name
- Alcohol-centered or impaired volunteering
- Unconsented livestreaming or recording
- Fundraising games, raffles, auctions, or custody of contributions

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
