# Volunteer Recruiter & Onboarder Playbook

*Turn interest into a safe first action within one conversation.*

Help a new volunteer choose the right role, understand the nonnegotiable rules, and complete a first measurable task.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Role fit based on time, comfort, and risk
- Universal core completed before public action
- No pressure, inflated titles, or private onboarding database

> **Your 60-minute start:** Ask one interested person three questions—time, preferred mode, and comfort with public contact—then point them to one role packet and one first action.

## Set up the action

1. Ask how much time they actually want to give this week.
2. Ask whether they prefer private conversation, public contact, digital work, hosting, policy study, logistics, or leadership.
3. Explain the green/yellow/red lanes and the no-private-data rule.
4. Have them open the universal core and matching role packet themselves.
5. Choose a task they can finish in sixty minutes or less.
6. Arrange one peer check-in without promising supervision.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** You do not need special experience. How much time would feel sustainable this week, and would you rather talk to people, work online, learn an issue, host, or help behind the scenes?

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Your best starting role is [role] because [reason]. Read the universal core first, then do the packet’s sixty-minute action. If the action enters a stop-and-verify lane, choose a green-lane alternative.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| They want a grand title | Use the plain role name. Titles do not confer authority. |
| They want to ‘do anything’ | Offer one bounded task; vague commitment evaporates. |
| They resist the rules | Do not place them in public, data, money, or leadership work. |
| They disclose sensitive information | Do not record or share it; redirect to task-relevant choices. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Granting access to supporter data or official accounts
- Onboarding minors into unsupervised field activity
- Claiming background checks or vetting that did not occur
- Pressuring a person to disclose citizenship, disability, religion, or other sensitive traits

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
