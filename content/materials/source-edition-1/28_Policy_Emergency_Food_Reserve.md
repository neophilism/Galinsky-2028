# Emergency Food Reserve Policy Action Kit

*Preserve safe surplus food and rotate it into established food-aid channels.*

Explain the Restore Natural Abundance proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The reserve would acquire safe but commercially rejected produce, preserve it through validated methods such as solar-assisted dehydration, store it under audited conditions, and rotate inventory into existing food-aid markets before quality declines.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

A large share of U.S. food is lost or wasted while disasters expose fragile supply chains. Cosmetic standards, oversupply, logistics, and shelf-life can discard edible food, but unsafe or spoiled food must never enter a reserve.

1. Contract for produce that is edible, traceable, unexpired, and rejected for a qualifying commercial reason—not food that failed safety controls.
2. Use licensed facilities, preventive controls, validated time/temperature and dehydration processes, allergen management, testing, packaging, labeling, and lot traceability.
3. Store inventory by first-expiring-first-out rules with environmental monitoring, recall capability, pest control, and independent audits.
4. Rotate stock through established food-aid procurement before expiration rather than treating vulnerable people as a dumping channel.
5. Measure nutrition, waste diversion, energy/water use, spoilage, logistics, disaster readiness, operating revenue, and public cost.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Food safety and dignity before waste reduction
- No donation of spoiled, recalled, adulterated, temperature-abused, or untraceable food
- Validated shelf life and nutrition, not assumptions about solar dehydration
- Fair procurement that does not undercut growers or local emergency-food systems
- Pilot by commodity and climate before national scale

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say all wasted food is edible or recoverable.
- Do not repeat ‘40 percent’ without noting it is an estimate and varies by definition/year.
- Do not collect, process, preserve, or distribute food as a campaign activity.
- Do not promise no net taxpayer cost before a pilot demonstrates revenue and loss rates.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is cosmetically imperfect food always safe? | No. Appearance and safety are different. Every lot still needs traceability and applicable safety controls. |
| Why sell rotated stock through food aid? | A dependable purchaser can rotate inventory before it ages, but contracts must protect nutrition, dignity, and existing providers. |
| Can solar tunnels meet food-safety standards? | Only validated designs and processes in licensed operations should be used. Climate, commodity, contamination, and moisture control matter. |
| How does the buildout repay? | The platform expects operating revenue to recoup initial infrastructure, a claim that pilots must test. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Volunteer with established food banks under their rules, separately from campaign persuasion.
2. Host a systems discussion with growers, grocers, food banks, tribal/rural communities, food-safety experts, emergency managers, and processors.
3. Use USDA’s estimate with its date and definition.
4. Ask what pass/fail thresholds a commodity pilot needs.
