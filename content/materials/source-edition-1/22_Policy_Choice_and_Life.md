# Choice & Life Policy Action Kit

*A long-horizon research goal for ending pregnancy without ending developing life.*

Explain the Complete the Revolution proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** Choice & Life proposes a major public research program toward ectogenesis—eventually transferring a pregnancy to an external gestation system—while preserving a woman’s decision to end pregnancy and seeking a path that does not end developing life.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

The platform treats bodily autonomy and concern for developing human life as real moral claims and asks whether future biotechnology can reduce the conflict rather than force a permanent political choice between them.

1. Fund transparent, staged basic and translational research across maternal-fetal medicine, neonatology, bioengineering, surgery, immunology, ethics, and long-term development.
2. Begin from current limited research in supporting extremely premature animal fetuses; do not treat complete ectogenesis or safe early-pregnancy transfer as available.
3. Require independent scientific, ethical, disability-rights, reproductive-rights, child-welfare, privacy, and regulatory oversight at every gate.
4. Advance only when evidence meets stringent safety, consent, long-term follow-up, and comparative-risk thresholds.
5. If an industry eventually emerges, use fixed public-return terms without government ownership or control of companies.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No claim that complete ectogenesis exists today
- No human use without lawful consent, validated safety, FDA oversight, research ethics review, and long-term outcome evidence
- No coercion of pregnant people, embryos/fetuses, intended parents, adoptees, disabled people, or research participants
- Explicit rules for parentage, custody, consent withdrawal, access, privacy, equity, failure, and child welfare before clinical use
- Scientific failure remains possible; research funding is not a promise of deployment

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say a fetus can currently be removed at any stage and gestated to term.
- Do not describe animal studies as proof of human safety.
- Do not tell anyone what medical or pregnancy decision to make.
- Do not shame, label, photograph, or target people seeking or opposing abortion care.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Does the technology exist? | No. Researchers have demonstrated limited extrauterine support in animal models and continue to study extremely premature support. Complete human ectogenesis and safe transfer from early pregnancy are not available. |
| Would this replace current reproductive law now? | No. The proposal is a research goal; current rights, medical standards, and law remain until any future technology is proven and legislation is enacted. |
| Who bears the risk? | That is central. The program would need consent, independent oversight, long-term child outcomes, maternal safety, failure protocols, and equitable-access rules before human use. |
| Is success guaranteed? | No. It is a high-risk scientific undertaking. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Lead with the technology-readiness disclaimer every time.
2. Host a structured listening session with reproductive-rights, pro-life, maternal-health, neonatology, disability, adoption, bioethics, and child-welfare perspectives.
3. Share only peer-reviewed or official regulatory sources and state the species/stage studied.
4. Do not organize at clinics, hospitals, or private homes; protect patients and staff from pressure.
