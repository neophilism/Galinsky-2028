# Campaign & Pillar Messaging Guide

*One voice, many volunteers, no invented promises.*

Provide a durable message architecture, exact accuracy boundaries, pillar bridges, and ready-to-use language for the full platform.

> **For:** Every volunteer who explains, writes, posts, presents, designs, recruits, canvasses, calls, texts, or answers questions

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The campaign message

> **One sentence:** Phillip Galinsky’s independent 2028 campaign proposes that America invest in people, land, technology, and peace under fixed public rules—earning value back without taking new control over private life.

> **Thirty seconds:** American politics keeps offering false choices: security or humanity, prosperity or a living land, help now or fiscal discipline. This platform looks for structures where America wins together. Public capital builds capacity, repayment returns value to the country, rules are fixed and open, and government takes no hidden control over private life. The result is a program of justice, peace, independence, natural abundance, and an outward frontier.

Volunteers should not memorize every plank. Memorize the governing method, one pillar, one policy, two safeguards, and one honest open question.

## The four-part message frame

| Part | Question | Volunteer language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | What is failing or missing? | Name one specific public problem without catastrophizing or blaming a group. |
| Proposal | What would change? | Say ‘the campaign proposes’ and describe the mechanism, not just the aspiration. |
| Safeguard | What prevents abuse or wishful thinking? | Name repayment, fixed rules, consent, no-control, non-displacement, or a scientific/legal gate. |
| Next step | What can this person do now? | Read the official text, ask a hard question, share accurately, volunteer, or donate through the official form. |

> **Best proof of seriousness:** Name what the proposal does not yet answer. Acknowledging an open cost, legal, scientific, or implementation question makes the defined mechanism more—not less—credible.

## The four governing principles

| Principle | Use when | Accuracy guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| America Wins Together | Someone assumes every policy requires a winner and loser. | Do not claim every tradeoff disappears; show the mechanism that changes incentives. |
| Government Nonintervention | A voluntary agreement needs a safe harbor. | Nonintervention is not immunity from fraud, safety, civil-rights, or other public-interest law. |
| Generational Land Release | Discussing permanent grants from a finite public estate. | No parcel is currently available and no generation may spend the whole inheritance. |
| Upside Without Control | Public capital or permission creates private returns. | Economic return without board seats does not eliminate ordinary regulation, contracts, or enforcement. |

## Claim levels

| Level | Meaning | Words to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed fact | Supported by a current primary source with scope and date. | ‘The agency reports…’ ‘In this study population…’ |
| Campaign interpretation | The platform’s explanation of why the fact matters. | ‘The campaign argues…’ |
| Proposal | A future legal or institutional mechanism. | ‘The platform proposes…’ ‘The law would…’ |
| Projection | An expected outcome dependent on assumptions. | ‘The proposal is designed to…’ ‘A pilot would test whether…’ |
| Personal view | The volunteer’s own reason or judgment. | ‘What persuades me is…’ |

> **Never collapse levels:** A problem being real does not prove the campaign’s mechanism will solve it. A promising mechanism is not an enacted benefit. A study is not an endorsement.

## Tone and vocabulary

| Prefer | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| independent campaign | outside/outsider by itself | ‘Independent’ describes ballot/political alignment; ‘outsider’ can imply lack of competence. |
| proposal, mechanism, safeguard | promise, guaranteed, solved | The platform still requires law, evidence, and implementation. |
| people who disagree | enemies, traitors, sheep | Persuasion requires dignity and democratic legitimacy. |
| people experiencing… | labels that reduce people to status | Person-first language protects dignity. |
| primary source with date/scope | viral statistic | A sourced limitation is stronger than an unsourced dramatic claim. |
| I don’t know | a confident guess | Accuracy is a campaign asset. |

## Bridging common concerns

| Concern | Bridge |
| --- | --- |
| ‘This is too ambitious.’ | ‘It is ambitious. That is why the platform uses pilots, fixed rules, independent scoring, milestones, and stop conditions rather than a blank check.’ |
| ‘Government will control everything.’ | ‘The recurring design is public return without board seats, vetoes, or arbitrary official discretion, while ordinary law still applies.’ |
| ‘There is no free money.’ | ‘Agreed. The platform calls for repayment, portfolio pricing, and independent scoring. The open question is whether each mechanism can meet that discipline.’ |
| ‘This is not legal now.’ | ‘Correct. It is a presidential platform proposing legislation and, for some planks, state or international participation.’ |
| ‘The science is not ready.’ | ‘For Choice & Life and several frontier proposals, that is explicitly true. The immediate proposal is staged research with safety and ethics gates, not deployment.’ |
| ‘Independent candidates cannot win.’ | ‘Ballot access and electoral support have to be built; neither is assumed. The reason to volunteer is to test whether these ideas can earn that support.’ |

## Correction protocol

1. Stop repeating the claim.
2. Open the official platform or primary source and identify the exact mismatch.
3. Correct the same audience and channel as visibly as practical: what was wrong, what is accurate, and the source.
4. If an official asset is wrong, unpublish or label it until corrected; do not let distribution continue.
5. Record the correction through the official volunteer form so related material can be checked.
6. Do not attack the person who found the error.

> **Correction script:** I stated [incorrect claim]. The accurate statement is [correct claim with scope/date]. The source is [official link]. I’m correcting it because campaign volunteers should make the argument with the facts intact.

## Ready-to-use general copy

> **Short social post:** I’m volunteering for Phillip Galinsky’s independent 2028 campaign because it tries to replace false choices with mechanisms we can test: public investment that builds, returns value, follows fixed rules, and takes no hidden control. Read the proposals at galinsky2028.us—and bring your hardest question.

> **Conversation opener:** May I show you one unusual policy idea? I’ll tell you what it proposes, its main safeguard, and the biggest unanswered question I see.

> **Volunteer invitation:** If you have more time than money, choose one self-directed action: share one accurate link, learn one policy, host four people, make five manual calls through an approved system, or help with a practical skill. Start with the Universal Volunteer Core at galinsky2028.us.

> **Donation invitation:** If time is scarce and the platform earns your support, use the official donation form reached from galinsky2028.us. Volunteers do not accept or process contributions.

## Message quality checklist

- [ ] The audience can tell I am a volunteer.
- [ ] I used one problem, one mechanism, and one safeguard.
- [ ] I called future policy a proposal.
- [ ] Each number has a current primary source, date, and scope.
- [ ] I did not promise eligibility, technology, savings, ballot status, or success.
- [ ] I left room for disagreement and an easy exit.
- [ ] I offered exactly one next step.
- [ ] The disclaimer and official link are intact where required.
