# SPISA & Eternal Liberty Space Program Action Kit

*Finance long-horizon space enterprise while carrying enforceable civil rights with it.*

Explain the Fill the Cosmos with Earthlife proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** Space Program Income Sharing Agreements would provide interest-free public capital with a fixed surcharge and income-contingent repayment to companies developing technologies for durable human and Earth-life expansion, conditioned on enforceable civil-rights protections.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Deep-space and life-support ventures have long timelines, technical failure risk, and uncertain early revenue. At the same time, remote settlements and company-controlled infrastructure can leave people without practical access to rights or courts.

1. Use open, milestone-based competitions for defined technology goals rather than favored-company deals.
2. Provide interest-free capital with a fixed surcharge repaid as a share of income only after qualifying earnings.
3. Price a diversified portfolio so successful ventures cover failures; use independent technical and actuarial scoring.
4. Condition support and relevant contracts on enforceable U.S. civil-rights, labor, due-process, privacy, safety, and access-to-counsel protections for people under company control.
5. Apply public returns first as legislated to debt reduction and later public purposes, with transparent accounting and no government board control.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No territorial sovereignty claim; treaty and international-law compliance
- No waiver of fundamental rights through employment, passage, housing, life support, or click-through contract
- Independent safety, planetary-protection, biosafety, mission-continuity, labor, and technical review
- Portfolio loss limits, milestones, clawbacks for fraud, and termination rights
- Jurisdiction, emergency authority, communications delay, evidence, courts, remedies, and counsel must work in practice

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not promise profit, repayment, a settlement date, a job, passage, citizenship, or land.
- Do not say one nation owns or can grant title to celestial territory.
- Do not imply Earth-life introduction is appropriate on a world that may host indigenous life.
- Do not call contractual rights enforceable until jurisdiction and remedies exist.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why an income share instead of a normal loan? | Repayment begins with revenue rather than fixed dates, which may fit long development cycles; a fixed surcharge and portfolio pricing constrain the obligation. |
| Can U.S. rights apply far from Earth? | Contracts, licensing, registration, funding conditions, and jurisdiction can carry obligations, but enforcement under delay and dependence needs new law and international coordination. |
| Does ‘Earthlife’ mean contaminating other worlds? | It must not. Planetary protection and any evidence of indigenous life come before propagation. |
| Who controls the companies? | The platform’s Upside Without Control principle rejects government board seats, vetoes, and corporate direction while preserving regulation and enforceable funding terms. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Use NASA’s commercial-space work to explain the existing public-private baseline without implying endorsement.
2. Host a rights-under-dependence exercise: air, water, shelter, work, speech, due process, privacy, disability access, and emergency evacuation.
3. Invite space-law, labor, civil-rights, planetary-protection, biosafety, technical, and finance experts to red-team the model.
4. Keep timelines and technical readiness explicitly uncertain.
