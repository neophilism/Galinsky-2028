# Great Undertakings Policy Action Kit

*Make peace profitable through jointly owned productive assets.*

Explain the Peace & Prosperity proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** Great Undertakings would create commercially viable assets with ownership, operations, and benefits distributed across potential rival countries, making war destroy the aggressor’s own property, revenue, and livelihoods.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Ordinary trade can be abandoned when leaders think conquest is more valuable. The platform seeks a deeper form of interdependence built around productive assets rather than fragile transactions.

1. Identify projects with real commercial value and operations on both countries’ soil.
2. Test that ownership, jobs, supply, and benefits are genuinely distributed rather than cosmetic.
3. Reject projects that create a coercive strategic dependency or require permanent subsidy.
4. Fix the public’s economic return in advance without a board seat, veto, or political control of the company.
5. Stress-test the project against changes of administration, sanctions, conflict, corruption, and supply interruption.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Commercial viability without permanent subsidy
- No single-point dependency that lets either side coerce the other
- Transparent ownership and anti-corruption review
- Public return without government corporate control
- Exit, security, labor, environmental, and dispute-resolution terms fixed before investment

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say commerce makes war impossible.
- Do not describe an unnamed foreign government or company as a partner.
- Do not promise a project before security and commercial review.
- Do not minimize human rights, labor, corruption, or supply-chain concerns.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Isn’t this just trade? | It is meant to be harder to unwind: productive property, operations, revenue, and jobs are owned across both sides. |
| Does it create dependence? | A project fails the platform’s test if it creates a coercive strategic dependency. |
| Why public investment? | The proposal treats the public as a silent economic partner and uses patient capital for assets whose peace value and return unfold over time. |
| Can it guarantee peace? | No. It is one incentive layer alongside defense, diplomacy, alliances, and law. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Use a hypothetical example clearly labeled hypothetical—such as shared critical-material processing—to test the five project criteria.
2. Ask business, labor, security, and human-rights participants to red-team the same project.
3. Share the difference between distributed interdependence and one-sided dependency.
4. Do not name a project as endorsed unless it appears in the official platform.
