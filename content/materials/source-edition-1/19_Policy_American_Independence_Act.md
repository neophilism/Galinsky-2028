# American Independence Act Policy Action Kit

*Earn a small-farm stake and rebuild the material basis of independence.*

Explain the Complete the Revolution proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The American Independence Act would release a fixed generational share of suitable unused federal land for independent small farms, pair working acreage with restored forest, and finance essential infrastructure under fixed repayment rules.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

High land and infrastructure costs make independent farming unreachable for many people, while concentration can leave families dependent on employers, landlords, banks, benefits systems, and distant food chains.

1. Inventory only suitable, legally available federal parcels after tribal consultation, environmental review, water analysis, contamination review, and existing-rights screening.
2. Release a fixed fraction for the current generation while reserving the rest for future generations.
3. Use transparent eligibility and performance standards for earning outright title through genuine independent farming.
4. Pair each farm with a matching restoration area and finance mills, storage, market access, and risk-pooling infrastructure.
5. Redirect a portion of existing farm support toward small independent farms and price repayment across the portfolio.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No use of tribal lands, treaty rights, sacred sites, valid existing rights, parks, refuges, or unsuitable/contaminated land
- Water, habitat, fire, climate, and carrying-capacity analysis before release
- Fixed public formulas rather than official favoritism
- Anti-speculation, residency/stewardship, succession, title, and failure rules established by law
- Every generation retains an opening

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not tell anyone land is currently available through this proposal.
- Do not call all federal land unused or suitable.
- Do not promise a parcel, title, subsidy, loan, crop result, or income.
- Do not erase the Homestead Act’s displacement of Native peoples or repeat it as a model without safeguards.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is this free land? | The proposal describes title earned through work and compliance, not an immediate giveaway. Exact eligibility and title conditions would require law. |
| How much land is available? | No final acreage is established. Suitability and exclusions must come before any estimate. |
| Does this threaten public lands? | The generational rule and legal exclusions are meant to preserve the public estate, but the inventory and conservation design require scrutiny. |
| How does it pay back? | Infrastructure and risk support use loans or portfolio repayment; the platform also redirects part of existing farm subsidies. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Use a local land map only to ask questions, never to label a parcel available.
2. Invite beginning farmers, tribal governments, water experts, conservationists, rural lenders, and public-land users to a safeguards workshop.
3. Share official data on federal land and farm structure with dates.
4. Ask audiences to distinguish earned title, portfolio finance, and existing subsidies.
