# Strategic Aid Policy Action Kit

*Prevent future American costs at their source and require capital to return.*

Explain the Peace & Prosperity proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** Strategic Aid would use staged, auditable, state-contingent loans to address instability before it becomes a larger American security, migration, or supply-chain cost.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Waiting for foreign instability to mature can leave the United States paying more through emergency enforcement, conflict, displacement, and supply disruption. Traditional grants can also create weak accountability and dependency.

1. Select only projects tied to a concrete preventable American cost and a viable local institution.
2. Use loans rather than grants, with payments indexed to the borrowing country’s economic performance.
3. Release funds in stages against audited milestones and enforceable commercial rules.
4. Prohibit projects that compete with an American company or move an American job overseas.
5. Recycle repayments through an existing development-finance vehicle into future stabilization investments.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Auditable institutions and milestone disbursement
- State-contingent repayment that falls during contraction and rises with growth
- No blank checks, permanent subsidy, or hidden security commitment
- Labor, environmental, anti-corruption, debt-sustainability, and human-rights review
- Transparent measurement of the avoided American cost

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not call every foreign-aid program an investment.
- Do not claim repayment is guaranteed.
- Do not promise that a loan alone prevents migration, terrorism, or instability.
- Do not imply the United States controls another country’s policy.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why not grants? | The platform prefers capital that returns and aligns repayment with success, while recognizing that project selection and risk still matter. |
| What happens in a recession? | Payments would adjust automatically with the borrowing economy rather than forcing a fixed payment through contraction. |
| Could this export U.S. jobs? | The proposal bars investments that compete with an American company or move an American job overseas. |
| Who administers it? | The platform points to the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation as an existing vehicle. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Explain one plausible project only as an example, then test it against job, corruption, repayment, and rights safeguards.
2. Invite diaspora, development-finance, security, labor, and migration experts to critique the model.
3. Share the distinction among grants, conventional debt, and state-contingent loans.
4. Avoid attributing a crisis to a single cause or stigmatizing migrants and foreign communities.
