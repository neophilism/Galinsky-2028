# Small-Dollar Fundraising Playbook

*Invite lawful support while keeping every contribution inside the official system.*

Make honest personal fundraising asks and host no-cost conversations without taking custody of money or making compliance judgments.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- All contributions use the current official form
- No cash, checks, card data, pass-through contributions, promises, or tax claims
- Donors decide freely and review required eligibility attestations themselves

> **Your 60-minute start:** Send three personal messages explaining why you volunteer and linking to the official donation form reached from galinsky2028.us.

## Set up the action

1. Tell your own truthful reason for supporting the campaign.
2. Link only to the current official donation form; inspect the domain before sending.
3. Ask for a voluntary amount without suggesting access, favors, services, or pressure.
4. Let donors read and answer the form’s eligibility and source-of-funds attestations themselves.
5. Do not enter information for a donor or watch them type payment data.
6. Track only the anonymous count of asks; do not maintain donor lists or guessed amounts.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** I’m volunteering because [personal reason]. If the platform speaks to you and you want to help a volunteer-only campaign build capacity, the official site has a donation form. Any amount is optional; reading or sharing is also valuable.

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Please use the official form and answer its eligibility questions yourself. I can’t accept funds, process a contribution, or advise you on limits.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Someone offers cash or a check | Decline custody and direct them to the official form. |
| They ask the legal limit | Use the current official form/FEC guidance; do not quote an old cycle’s number. |
| They want to reimburse your event costs | Decline and do not route it through you. |
| They ask if it is tax-deductible | Say campaign contributions generally are not charitable tax deductions and direct tax questions to a qualified adviser. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Cash, checks, crypto, gift cards, payment credentials, or contributions in another person’s name
- Corporate, labor-organization, federal-contractor, or foreign-national source questions that the official process has not cleared
- Raffles, auctions, matching schemes, prizes, or sales
- Fundraising commissions, quotas tied to status, or promised access

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
