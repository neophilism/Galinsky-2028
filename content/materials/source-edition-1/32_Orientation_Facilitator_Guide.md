# Volunteer Orientation Facilitator Guide

*Run a complete orientation even when no paid supervisor is available.*

Provide a word-for-word facilitation plan, exercises, checks for understanding, accessibility practices, and an answer key for live or self-directed orientation.

> **For:** Event hosts, recruiters, local captains, and any volunteer facilitating the campaign orientation deck

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Before the session

- [ ] Use the current orientation deck and Universal Core with matching edition dates.
- [ ] Confirm an accessible room or platform, captions, keyboard navigation, readable materials, breaks, and a camera-free way to participate.
- [ ] State that no one must disclose political affiliation, citizenship, disability, medical history, criminal history, or other sensitive facts.
- [ ] Do not record without specific opt-in consent from every participant.
- [ ] Share only the official signup form; do not build a personal attendance list.
- [ ] Prepare one role card and one policy kit for each planned exercise.
- [ ] Know the emergency exits and local emergency number.

## Sixty-minute run of show

| Time | Slide/topic | Facilitator action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Welcome | State purpose, volunteer status, conduct rules, accessibility options, and no-recording default. |
| 5–12 | Campaign method | Teach the one-sentence message and four governing principles. |
| 12–22 | Green / yellow / red | Use three scenarios; participants identify the lane and why. |
| 22–30 | Truth and persuasion | Practice problem → proposal → safeguard → next step. |
| 30–38 | Privacy, money, automation | Show the hard stops and call-bank party rule. |
| 38–47 | Choose a role | Participants answer time / mode / regulated-training questions. |
| 47–55 | First-action practice | Each person writes and rehearses a 60-minute action. |
| 55–60 | Commit and close | Run the knowledge check; send everyone to their own official role packet/form. |

## Opening script

> **Read aloud:** Welcome. This is a volunteer-built independent presidential campaign. Today is not about memorizing every policy or winning an argument. It is about learning how to take one useful action without needing a supervisor—and how to recognize the few moments when stopping is the responsible action. We tell the truth, protect consent and private data, use the official campaign system, and never guess where law or safety is involved.

- Invite pronouns only if people wish to share; never require them.
- Ask participants to signal any access need privately or through the platform.
- Establish ‘step up / step back,’ one microphone, no personal attacks, no unconsented screenshots, and the right to pass.

## Exercise 1: choose the lane

| Scenario | Correct lane | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A volunteer sends an official policy link to five friends with personal notes. | Green | Personal, unpaid, public information, no private database. |
| Eight volunteers manually call a campaign list at a call-bank party using the current script/tool. | Yellow | Allowed through an activated campaign system; humans call one at a time and honor opt-outs. |
| A volunteer imports 5,000 scraped numbers into an autodialer with a cloned candidate voice. | Red | Automation, consent, impersonation, data, and telemarketing law risks. |
| A volunteer downloads a petition form from an old website and begins collecting signatures. | Red | Ballot rules and forms are state-, election-, and date-specific. |
| A designer drafts a poster from public campaign material and submits editable files without publishing. | Green | Bounded draft; public sources; no official deployment. |
| A captain signs a free venue agreement ‘for the campaign.’ | Red | Even a zero-dollar agreement can bind the committee and create liability. |

## Exercise 2: fix the claim

Read each claim. Ask participants to replace it with language that separates fact, proposal, and uncertainty.

| Overclaim | Grounded version |
| --- | --- |
| ‘The campaign will give you a farm.’ | ‘The campaign proposes legislation through which qualified people could earn title to suitable designated land under fixed rules; no application or parcel exists today.’ |
| ‘Artificial wombs will end abortion.’ | ‘The platform proposes a high-risk research program toward ectogenesis. Complete human ectogenesis is not available and success is not guaranteed.’ |
| ‘Our defense will stop every missile.’ | ‘The proposal seeks layered defenses that reduce the chance an attack succeeds; it does not promise perfect protection.’ |
| ‘Food waste will pay for the reserve.’ | ‘The platform expects operating revenue to recoup buildout, which pilots must test under food-safety and market constraints.’ |

## Exercise 3: choose the role

1. Ask: ‘How much time can you give this week without resentment?’
2. Ask: ‘Would you rather talk privately, meet the public, work online, host, lead, learn policy, protect election access, or build behind the scenes?’
3. Ask: ‘Are you prepared to wait for training before doing a regulated activity?’
4. Route to one playbook only.
5. Have the participant state the first action, quantity, date, stop condition, and reporting method.

> **Facilitator rule:** Do not reward grand ambition with a grand title. Reward a finished, repeatable action with the next bounded responsibility.

## Knowledge check

1. What three categories of action does the campaign use?
2. What should you say before describing a future policy?
3. Where should supporter information be collected?
4. Can volunteers hold a party where people manually call an approved list?
5. Can that party use an autodialer or prerecorded/artificial voice?
6. What do you do when a person opts out?
7. When may a volunteer circulate an independent-candidate petition?
8. May a volunteer accept cash or a check for the campaign?
9. What is the first response to immediate danger?
10. What five parts define a first action?

| # | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Green, yellow, and red. |
| 2 | ‘The campaign proposes’ or equivalent future-tense language. |
| 3 | Only in the current official campaign form/system. |
| 4 | Yes, if the system, list, script, time, and state instructions are active; people call one at a time. |
| 5 | No, not without written campaign and legal approval. |
| 6 | Stop the contact and record do-not-contact immediately in the official system. |
| 7 | Only with the exact dated State Activation Sheet, official form, completed training, and custody process. |
| 8 | No; direct the donor to the official form. |
| 9 | Call 911 or the appropriate local emergency number. |
| 10 | One action, quantity, owner, date, and stop condition/reporting path. |

## Closing script

> **Read aloud:** Choose the smallest action you can finish. Open your role packet before you begin. If the action is green, act. If it is yellow, use the current official system. If it is red or unclear, stop and choose useful work that is safely green. Your credibility is more valuable than one extra call, signature, post, dollar, or event.

- [ ] Each participant opened their role packet.
- [ ] Each participant named one measurable action and stop condition.
- [ ] No private attendance list remains.
- [ ] Questions that require policy/legal review were submitted without delaying green-lane work.

## Optional ninety-minute extension

- Add 10 minutes for policy-kit practice in pairs.
- Add 10 minutes for accessibility and de-escalation scenarios.
- Add 10 minutes for a mock call, door, or event opening with peer feedback.

Do not lengthen the legal lecture. People learn boundaries by classifying realistic scenarios and practicing the exact safe alternative.
