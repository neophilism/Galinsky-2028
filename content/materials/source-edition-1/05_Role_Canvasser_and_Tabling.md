# Canvassing & Tabling Playbook

*Meet the public safely, respectfully, and lawfully.*

Hold short conversations in lawful public or permitted spaces and direct interested people to the official campaign site.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Permission-based contacts
- Buddy-system field safety
- No trespass, mailbox placement, obstruction, or unapproved data collection

> **Your 60-minute start:** Choose a lawful public location, recruit a buddy, confirm any venue rules, and practice a fifteen-second opener before going out.

## Set up the action

1. Verify that canvassing or tabling is lawful at the exact place and time; private property requires permission.
2. Use a buddy and agree on start, end, meeting point, and emergency check-in.
3. Bring only current, unaltered campaign materials with the disclaimer intact.
4. Dress for weather, carry water, and keep walkways, ramps, doors, and polling access completely clear.
5. Use the official QR/link; do not use a personal sign-up sheet.
6. Count contacts anonymously and leave the area cleaner than you found it.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** Hi—I’m a volunteer with Phillip Galinsky’s independent presidential campaign. May I give you a fifteen-second summary of one proposal?

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Thanks. If you want the full text or want to take action, the official place is galinsky2028.us. I won’t collect your information here.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| A property manager asks you to leave | Leave immediately, even if you think you are right; verify later. |
| A person is angry | Create distance, end the conversation, and move toward your buddy or a staffed public place. |
| Someone wants several flyers | Give a reasonable number only if materials are authorized; do not place items in or on mailboxes. |
| Weather or visibility changes | End early; no contact target is worth unsafe conditions. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Entering posted or closed property
- Working alone after dark or in an unsafe area
- Blocking access or approaching people who cannot comfortably disengage
- Canvassing at a polling place without a current election-law module

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
