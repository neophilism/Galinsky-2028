# Digital Ambassador Playbook

*Share accurate campaign ideas online without becoming spammy or misleading.*

Create a steady stream of truthful, human-scale posts that lead people back to the official platform.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Original personal commentary tied to official sources
- Respectful replies and prompt corrections
- No paid boosts, deceptive media, bots, or coordinated harassment

> **Your 60-minute start:** Choose one official policy page, write a three-sentence personal reaction, link the source, and invite one sincere question.

## Set up the action

1. Use your own account; do not imitate an official account.
2. Read the entire policy section before summarizing it.
3. Write the claim, then attach the official link and a current primary source for any number.
4. Add alt text and captions where the platform supports them.
5. Review the post as if an opponent were checking every word.
6. Publish once; engage selectively and calmly.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** I’m volunteering for Phillip Galinsky’s independent 2028 campaign because the platform asks a useful question: how can public investment solve a problem, return value, and avoid new control over private life? Here’s the proposal I’m examining today: [official link]. What would you want tested before it became law?

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Good question. The platform proposes [accurate one-sentence summary]. It does not yet settle [open implementation detail]. The official text is here: [link].

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| A factual error is found | Correct it visibly and promptly; do not quietly erase the record unless safety requires deletion. |
| A troll provokes you | Do not quote-post for ridicule. Mute, block, or report. |
| A post gets unusual bot-like amplification | Do not coordinate with it or claim support; report suspicious activity through the platform and official campaign form. |
| You want to use a trending image or music | Use only material you own, have licensed, or the campaign has approved. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Paid promotion or boosts
- AI impersonation, synthetic endorsement, or fake event imagery
- Automation that creates accounts, follows, likes, replies, or messages
- Sharing private supporter information or internal materials

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
