# Personal Network Organizer Playbook

*Turn trust you already have into careful, personal invitations.*

Help friends, relatives, coworkers, neighbors, and community contacts discover one campaign idea and choose their own next step.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Five to twenty individual conversations per week
- No bulk spam and no private database
- A clear invitation to read, volunteer, or donate through the official site

> **Your 60-minute start:** Make a private list of ten people you genuinely know, choose one issue relevant to each, and send five personal messages.

## Set up the action

1. Choose ten real relationships—not scraped contacts or a purchased list.
2. Write one sentence about the concern each person has previously expressed; do not infer sensitive traits.
3. Match one platform idea to that concern and open the official issue page.
4. Send individual messages at a humane pace. Personalize; do not paste a wall of text.
5. Record anonymous totals only.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** Hi [first name]—I’ve been reading Phillip Galinsky’s independent 2028 platform. One idea on [issue] made me think of our conversation about [shared concern]. Would you be open to a short link? No pressure at all.

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** Thanks for taking a look. What landed for you, and what felt unclear? I’m a volunteer, so if I don’t know an answer I’ll point you to the campaign’s published platform rather than guess.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| They do not reply | Do nothing. Silence is not consent to repeated messages. |
| They disagree | Ask one curious question, reflect their answer, and end warmly. |
| They want updates | Direct them to the current official signup form; do not add them to your own list. |
| They ask you to debate publicly | Choose a respectful one-to-one exchange or decline. Do not dogpile. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Sending the same unsolicited message at scale
- Contacting someone after an opt-out
- Using employer/customer/member data
- Promising access, favors, benefits, or policy outcomes

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
