# Universal Volunteer Core

*Read this before taking any public campaign action.*

A self-service operating manual for truthful, lawful, safe, and useful volunteer work in a decentralized independent presidential campaign.

> **For:** Every volunteer, organizer, host, captain, creative contributor, and campaign supporter

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Start here: the volunteer operating system

> **The one rule:** Tell the truth, protect people, and use only lawful methods. If a tactic depends on changing law, money, private data, automation, a permit, or an official campaign account, pause until the required verification exists.

This campaign is volunteer-built. That means you are trusted to act without waiting for a supervisor on ordinary, low-risk work—and expected to stop when a legal or safety boundary appears. The standard is not whether a tactic is clever. The standard is whether a reasonable person could verify what you did, whom you contacted, what you said, and whether consent was respected.

| Lane | You may do | What changes the lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green — act now | Share official public links; talk with people you know; host a small discussion; print unaltered official material; invite people to the official volunteer or donation form. | No private data collection, spending, automation, or claim of campaign authority. |
| Yellow — use the campaign system | Canvass, call, text, table, run an event, recruit, or submit creative work using an official list, script, form, and approved asset. | Use the current instructions; honor opt-outs; record only the requested minimum. |
| Red — stop and verify | Petition circulation, voter registration, paid media, mass calls/texts, contracts, fundraising custody, permits, data exports, public statements for the campaign. | A current state module, written authorization, or legal review is required. |

> **No supervisor available?:** A missing answer never turns a red-lane action green. Choose a green-lane action, document the unresolved question through the official volunteer form, and continue with work that cannot harm the campaign.

3. Choose one measurable action small enough to finish today: five personal messages, one public post, one ten-person invitation list, or one issue conversation.

## Truth, attribution, and message discipline

- Quote only words that appear in the current official platform, and keep enough context that the meaning is not distorted.
- For summaries, say ‘the campaign proposes’ or ‘the platform would.’ Distinguish the campaign’s argument from independently established fact.
- Use dated, primary sources for factual claims. If you cannot retrieve the source, remove the number rather than guessing.
- Never fabricate endorsements, poll results, ballot status, staffing, partnerships, scientific readiness, costs, savings, or legal authority.
- Do not present AI-generated text, images, audio, or video as a real person, event, endorsement, or campaign statement.
- Do not alter an official graphic in a way that changes its claim, disclaimer, date, or context.
- If speaking personally, say so. Do not use a title that implies authority to bind the campaign.

| If asked… | Say… |
| --- | --- |
| Is this official? | I’m a volunteer sharing the campaign’s published material. The current official position is at galinsky2028.us. |
| Can you promise this will happen? | No. It is a governing proposal that would require legislation, implementation, and public oversight. |
| What does it cost? | The platform describes repayment and portfolio rules, but I won’t invent a score. Independent scoring and implementation details would be required. |
| Is the candidate on my ballot? | Ballot access is state-specific and still must be verified. Please check your state election authority and the campaign’s current status. |

## Respectful persuasion

A useful conversation is not a contest. Ask permission, learn what the person cares about, offer one relevant idea, check understanding, and leave the relationship intact.

1. Open: ‘May I tell you about one idea from an independent presidential campaign?’
2. Ask: ‘What problem do you most want the next president to solve?’
3. Reflect: summarize their concern without arguing with it.
4. Connect: explain one proposal in no more than two sentences.
5. Check: ‘What part sounds promising or unclear?’
6. Answer only what you know. Use ‘I don’t know’ freely and point to the official platform.
7. Invite one next step: read, share, volunteer, or donate through the official site.
8. Thank them and end promptly if they decline.

> **De-escalation:** If someone becomes hostile: lower your voice, do not crowd them, do not touch them or their property, say ‘I’m going to end the conversation now,’ and leave. Call emergency services for immediate danger. Online, save evidence only if needed, block/report, and do not mobilize followers against the person.

## Privacy and digital security

- [ ] Use a unique password and multifactor authentication on every campaign-related account.
- [ ] Open campaign pages by typing the official domain; be cautious with login links sent by strangers.
- [ ] Collect supporter information only through the current official form. Never photograph sign-in sheets or copy data into a personal spreadsheet.
- [ ] Do not collect Social Security numbers, dates of birth, government IDs, full voter files, medical information, payment information, or immigration status.
- [ ] Do not upload supporter data, call lists, internal documents, or private conversations to a public AI tool.
- [ ] Share files as view-only unless editing is necessary; remove access when the task ends.
- [ ] Keep minors’ information out of personal devices and never arrange one-on-one unsupervised activity with a minor.
- [ ] If a device or account may be compromised, stop using it for campaign work, change credentials from a trusted device, and report through the official volunteer form.

> **Data minimization:** If the official campaign form is unavailable, distribute public information and count outcomes anonymously. Do not build a substitute contact list.

## Money, materials, and legal boundaries

- Unpaid personal volunteer services are generally not contributions under federal campaign-finance law, but money, goods, paid promotion, facilities, transportation for others, or professional work can create a contribution or reporting issue.
- Use the official donation form. Do not accept cash, checks, card numbers, cryptocurrency, gift cards, or donor login credentials.
- Never reimburse yourself, promise reimbursement, pool money, sign a contract, rent space, buy a list, or order materials in the campaign’s name without written authorization.
- Do not use an employer’s staff, equipment, paid time, postage, mailing list, or facilities for campaign activity. Incidental-use rules are technical; the simple campaign rule is to use your own device and your own time.
- Do not buy or boost political ads. Unpaid personal internet activity is different from paid placement.
- Do not imply that campaign contributions are tax-deductible. Do not run a raffle, lottery, auction, or prize promotion without written legal clearance.
- Foreign nationals may face restrictions on political spending and decision-making. No one should contribute or direct campaign strategy unless federal law permits it.
- Place the campaign disclaimer on official public communications when supplied. Never crop, cover, or shrink it beyond legibility.

> **Internal no-spend rule:** Assume personal spending is not authorized and will not be reimbursed. Ordinary personal internet access and truly incidental activity are fine; anything beyond that belongs in the stop-and-verify lane.

## Calling, texting, and automation

A group of volunteers may hold a social call-bank gathering. Calls must be placed by real people, one at a time, using a lawful campaign-provided list and current script, or by volunteers contacting people they personally know. A call-bank party does not authorize automated dialing, prerecorded audio, artificial voice, or mass messaging.

| Allowed with the stated condition | Not allowed without written campaign and legal approval |
| --- | --- |
| A volunteer manually calls one number, identifies the campaign, speaks live, and records the requested outcome. | Autodialers, predictive dialers, prerecorded calls, ringless voicemail, artificial or cloned voices. |
| A volunteer sends a personal message to someone they know and respects a decline. | Bulk texts, purchased/scraped numbers, repeated messaging after opt-out, or tools that disguise sender identity. |
| A group uses one current campaign script and immediately updates do-not-contact status. | Importing campaign lists into personal apps, sharing screenshots, or keeping a duplicate contact database. |

> **Universal opt-out:** If someone says stop, do not contact them again through that channel. Mark the opt-out immediately in the official system. If no official system is available, end the contact and do not retain or reuse the number.

## Safety, inclusion, and conduct

- Follow venue rules, public-access rules, permit conditions, and lawful instructions from election or public-safety officials.
- Never threaten, intimidate, stalk, harass, dox, discriminate, obstruct access, damage property, or interfere with another campaign’s event or materials.
- Never carry a weapon while volunteering, even where lawful, and never volunteer while impaired.
- Use a buddy system for canvassing, petitioning, late events, and unfamiliar locations. Share a check-in plan; leave any place that feels unsafe.
- Offer accessible routes, seating, captions or transcripts, readable type, and a way to participate without standing or speaking publicly.
- Do not pressure anyone for political support in exchange for help, services, food, employment, housing, rides, or access.
- Do not use slurs or speculate about a person’s race, ancestry, disability, religion, citizenship, gender, medical history, or criminal history.

> **Emergency rule:** For immediate danger or a medical emergency, call 911 or the appropriate local emergency number. Campaign reporting never replaces emergency services.

## Self-service decision tree

| Question | If yes | If no |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Could this action change someone’s legal rights, ballot eligibility, donation record, or contact consent? | Use the current official process; stop if it does not exist. | Continue. |
| Does it involve money, a contract, paid reach, private data, automation, a permit, or an official account? | Written authorization or an activated campaign tool is required. | Continue. |
| Am I certain the claim appears in the current platform or a cited primary source? | Use accurate context and date. | Remove the claim or say you do not know. |
| Could a reasonable person think I speak for or can bind the campaign? | Identify yourself as a volunteer and narrow the statement. | Continue. |
| Is anyone unsafe, pressured, impaired, or asking to be left alone? | Stop immediately and protect safety/consent. | Proceed respectfully. |

## Measure work without collecting people

The campaign needs enough information to learn, not enough to surveil. Unless the official campaign system requests more, record anonymous totals only.

| Record | Do not record |
| --- | --- |
| Date, role, general area, minutes volunteered, number attempted, number reached, number interested, number directed to official form, common question, safety/compliance issue. | Political opinions tied to names, screenshots of private messages, medical or immigration information, payment details, unverified allegations, or a shadow supporter list. |

A useful debrief is three lines: What happened? What did I learn? What will I change next time? Submit it through the current official volunteer form when available.
