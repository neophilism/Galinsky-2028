# RISA Policy Action Kit

*Voluntary work, learning, savings, restitution, and repayment during and after incarceration.*

Explain the Complete the Revolution proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** Rehabilitative Income Sharing Agreements would let incarcerated people voluntarily choose market-relevant learning or qualified work, earn release savings, support restitution and confinement costs, and make a limited post-release income share only while working.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Long prison terms can leave people idle, disconnected from lawful work, without savings, and vulnerable to rearrest. Taxpayers fund confinement while victims, families, and communities receive too little repair.

1. Offer voluntary RISA Learn and RISA Gain tracks as alternatives to existing low-skill assignments where applicable.
2. Use accredited, market-tested training and qualified work matched to a participant’s actual capability.
3. Divide earnings under law among fair pay, release savings, restitution/obligations, and a transparent contribution toward confinement/reentry costs.
4. After release, collect only a smaller capped income share in months the participant works, ending at a fixed term or repayment multiple.
5. Provide independent counsel, grievance rights, labor/safety protections, data privacy, licensing review, and reentry support.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Voluntary, informed consent without punishment for declining
- No forced labor, debt bondage, unfair wage suppression, or employer displacement
- Independent counsel and accessible grievance process
- Protected release savings and clear priority for victim restitution/child support as law decides
- No repayment during unemployment and a permanent end date/multiple

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not call incarcerated people ‘wasted talent’ while ignoring coercion and rights.
- Do not promise a job, license, early release, recidivism result, or debt amount.
- Do not repeat the platform’s national cost figure as the cost of every facility or person.
- Do not solicit personal criminal histories or victim information.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is this forced prison labor? | It must not be. The proposal says participation is voluntary and calls for a choice between learning and gain tracks, with earned savings and limited repayment. Strong statutory protections are essential. |
| Who benefits financially? | The design must specify fair compensation, release savings, restitution, confinement/reentry contributions, and public accounting—without private profiteering from coercion. |
| What if a person cannot find work after release? | The post-release share applies only in working months and must end under a clear cap. |
| Does training reduce recidivism? | Education and work can help, but program quality and selection matter. Do not attribute causation to one statistic. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Center formerly incarcerated people, victims, corrections staff, educators, labor, employers, families, and civil-rights advocates in the design discussion.
2. Compare the platform’s claims with the exact population and period in BJS studies.
3. Ask what wage, consent, grievance, savings, and licensing rules prevent exploitation.
4. Use person-first language and never expose anyone’s history without permission.
