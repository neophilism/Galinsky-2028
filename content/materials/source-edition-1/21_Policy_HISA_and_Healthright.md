# HISA & Healthright Policy Action Kit

*Finance care when it is needed and repay only while earning.*

Explain the Complete the Revolution proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** A Healthcare Income Sharing Agreement would finance needed care with an interest-free advance plus a fixed noncompounding surcharge, repaid as a capped share of income only in months the patient earns.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Medical bills can delay care and leave families with long-lived, compounding, or unaffordable debt. The platform seeks immediate access without a recurring premium and without bankruptcy over care.

1. Create a federal safe harbor for a defined HISA model that accepts every applicant and charges no interest, only a fixed disclosed surcharge.
2. Finance covered care at the time of need without an upfront patient charge or surprise balance under the approved contract.
3. Set repayment as a small capped share of income only while the borrower is earning; pause payments in hard times.
4. End repayment permanently when the principal and agreed surcharge are paid.
5. Establish provider eligibility, care standards, pricing, consumer disclosures, servicing, appeals, privacy, fraud prevention, and portfolio solvency rules.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No interest or compounding; fixed surcharge disclosed in advance
- Universal applicant acceptance within the protected model
- Income cap, hardship pause, definite payoff, and no medical lien or collection abuse
- Independent actuarial testing and clear treatment of low-income, disabled, chronically ill, and never-earning patients
- HISA is financing, not insurance; emergency access and patient protections remain essential

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say HISA exists nationwide or is currently guaranteed.
- Do not tell anyone to drop insurance, delay care, or sign a financial agreement.
- Do not promise every service has zero upfront cost before coverage rules exist.
- Do not give medical, insurance, credit, bankruptcy, or tax advice.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is it insurance? | No. The platform describes a financing agreement used when care is needed, not risk-pooling through recurring premiums. |
| What if a person has no income? | The proposal pauses payments when the borrower is not earning. Long-run portfolio design for people unable to earn requires explicit legislation and actuarial protection. |
| Why would a provider accept it? | A lawful financing vehicle could pay the provider while the patient repays over income; provider pricing and eligibility rules remain implementation questions. |
| Can the balance grow forever? | The proposal says no interest, a fixed surcharge, and a permanent end once the agreed amount is paid. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Begin with lived experience only when freely offered; never collect medical or debt details.
2. Compare HISA’s proposed financing features with official research on medical debt.
3. Host a safeguards discussion with patients, clinicians, hospitals, consumer advocates, disability advocates, actuaries, and health economists.
4. Use ‘would’ and ‘proposes’ in every public explanation.
