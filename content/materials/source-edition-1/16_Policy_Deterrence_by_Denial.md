# Deterrence by Denial Policy Action Kit

*Make attacks fail before retaliation becomes necessary.*

Explain the Peace & Prosperity proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The campaign proposes sustained investment in layered defensive technology—sensing, interception, cyber resilience, and industrial capacity—so an aggressor expects an attack to fail rather than profit.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Deterrence based mainly on threatened retaliation assumes leaders always believe the threat, calculate correctly, and maintain restraint through crisis and false alarm. The platform argues that protection should carry more of the burden.

1. Accelerate persistent sensing and command systems that detect threats early.
2. Build layered defenses against missiles, drones, cyberattack, and incursions across multiple phases and domains.
3. Expand test capacity, manufacturing depth, repair, stockpiles, and allied interoperability.
4. Use open competition, measurable tests, cybersecurity, and independent oversight to avoid vendor capture.
5. Keep constitutional war powers, civilian control, alliance commitments, arms-control obligations, and strategic-stability analysis in the design.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Defense is not claimed to be perfect or invulnerable
- Testing against realistic countermeasures and public accounting where security permits
- No claim that technology eliminates diplomacy, alliances, or the need for restraint
- Procurement diversity and failure-tolerant architecture
- No operational details shared by volunteers

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not promise an impenetrable shield.
- Do not imply retaliation, diplomacy, or alliances disappear overnight.
- Do not share sensitive technical speculation or target information.
- Do not turn disagreement into accusations of disloyalty.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Can any defense stop everything? | No. The proposal is layered risk reduction and a changed attacker calculation, not perfect protection. |
| Could defenses destabilize a nuclear relationship? | That risk must be analyzed alongside survivability, arms control, alliance effects, and adversary responses. The kit does not claim a simple answer. |
| Is this only missile defense? | No. The platform’s logic includes drones, sensing, cyber resilience, borders, logistics, and the industrial base. |
| How would success be judged? | By realistic test performance, capacity, resilience, cost exchange, coverage, and whether likely attacks can achieve their objective. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Use the two-minute explanation with veterans, engineers, emergency managers, and peace advocates, then ask what safeguards they require.
2. Share official descriptions of current defensive sensors to distinguish the existing baseline from the proposed acceleration.
3. Host a policy discussion on test standards and strategic stability.
4. Avoid operational claims; submit technical questions through the official volunteer form.
