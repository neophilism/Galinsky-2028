# The Cornerstone Policy Action Kit

*A new foundation when government deliberately destroys a lawful livelihood.*

Explain the Justice for All proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The Cornerstone would give identifiable workers directly displaced by an intentional government campaign against a lawful industry a collectively governed, commercially usable replacement opportunity.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Transition programs often diffuse help broadly even when a public policy intentionally concentrates loss on specific workers and communities. The platform argues that when destruction of a lawful industry is an objective or intended means of government action, the affected workers are owed a particular remedy.

1. Use fixed Department of Labor rules to certify the governmental action, evidence of intent, affected worksites and occupations, causal period, and directly displaced people.
2. Let eligible workers form voluntary one-member-one-vote Cornerstone Associations before an opportunity is allocated.
3. Invite participating states to offer commercial opportunities that are currently prohibited in selected districts and match claims by a sealed, public rule.
4. Preserve worker voting control while allowing outside capital to earn a return without buying the compact or governing the association.
5. Use a revolving fund for certification, formation, feasibility, licensing, and training; repay it from enterprise revenue under a fixed formula. A distinct, modest flat state share applies only to surplus above a protected financial baseline and funds state land acquisition.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Narrow trigger: intentional destruction or material contraction, not every economic transition
- Certification of direct causation and identifiable workers
- Voluntary membership, equal founding ownership, open books, and no forced relocation/retraining/debt
- The state surplus-share rate and negotiable terms must be published before matching; the state gains no vote or management control
- Independent safety, consumer, labor, environmental, and anti-corruption regulation
- The remedy is an enforceable opportunity, not a guaranteed profit

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not promise eligibility to every worker affected by market change.
- Do not imply the program restores the old job or guarantees success.
- Do not claim a state has offered any industry or district before legislation and public rules exist.
- Do not use workers’ hardship as a prop; ask permission before sharing personal stories.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why not ordinary retraining? | The proposal adds collective ownership and a usable commercial opportunity to training, because it treats intentional public harm as a debt to specific workers. |
| Who qualifies? | The proposed Department of Labor certification would require intent, direct causation, affected occupations/worksites, a causal period, and individual displacement. |
| Can investors take over? | No. Outside capital may receive returns, but workers retain voting control and the compact cannot be sold to investors. |
| Is profit guaranteed? | No. Government owes a usable field in which effort can matter, not commercial success. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Interview affected workers only with informed permission and let them review how their story will be used.
2. Compare the proposal with official economic-adjustment evaluations and ask what conventional programs miss.
3. Host a small discussion with labor, community college, small-business, and state-regulatory participants.
4. Share the precise trigger and the no-guarantee distinction.
