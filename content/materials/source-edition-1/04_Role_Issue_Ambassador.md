# Issue Ambassador Playbook

*Explain one proposal deeply, accurately, and without pretending every detail is settled.*

Become a reliable volunteer explainer for one policy while keeping the line between evidence, proposal, and personal opinion clear.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- A memorized 30-second explanation
- A sourced two-minute explanation
- Honest answers to predictable objections and a clear ‘I don’t know’

> **Your 60-minute start:** Pick one policy kit, practice its 30-second explanation aloud three times, and have one permission-based conversation.

## Set up the action

1. Read the current platform plank and its matching action kit.
2. Mark the proposal’s problem, mechanism, repayment/control rule, dependencies, and open questions.
3. Open every official reference you plan to cite and note its date.
4. Practice without jargon; define each acronym the first time.
5. Ask a skeptical friend to identify where you overclaimed.
6. Send people to the full official text.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** The campaign proposes [mechanism] to address [problem]. The distinctive safeguard is [fixed rule / repayment / no-control provision]. It would still require [legislation, testing, state participation, or international agreement].

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** That concern is fair. The platform’s answer is [published safeguard]. It does not by itself answer [unresolved detail], which would need public scoring, legislation, or implementation rules.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| You forget a detail | Say you do not know; look it up after the conversation. |
| Two sources conflict | Prefer the responsible primary agency and state the date/scope; do not average incompatible figures. |
| A listener treats a research goal as available technology | Correct the distinction immediately. |
| The topic becomes personal or traumatic | Do not interrogate. Acknowledge, avoid policy combat, and let the person end. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Inventing cost estimates, eligibility, dates, or legal authority
- Speaking as a licensed medical, legal, or financial professional unless you are one and the context is appropriate
- Targeting people based on sensitive personal data
- Turning a proposal into a guaranteed personal benefit

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
