# Restoration Homestead Act Policy Action Kit

*Earn title by restoring suitable degraded public land under strict standards.*

Explain the Restore Natural Abundance proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The Restoration Homestead Act would let qualified Americans live on selected degraded public parcels under low-impact rules, rebuild ecological function and food-producing diversity, and earn title after meeting standards written in law.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Some disturbed public lands need long-duration restoration, while land and housing costs leave many people without a path to ownership. The proposal trades verified restoration labor for a permanent stake.

1. Inventory legally eligible degraded parcels using tribal consultation, title and treaty review, contamination and unexploded-ordnance screening, water analysis, habitat/fire risk, access, and carrying capacity.
2. Release only a generational fraction and retain conservation corridors and future-generation reserves.
3. Select qualified applicants through fixed public rules and require a feasible restoration/living plan, training, monitoring, and financial resilience.
4. Allow temporary low-impact residence only under health, sanitation, water, fire, building, and emergency standards.
5. Award title only after independent verification of statutory ecological benchmarks; establish failure, exit, succession, resale, and anti-speculation rules.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- No self-selection or squatting on public land; only legislatively designated parcels
- No Native land, sacred site, valid existing right, park/refuge, critical habitat, hazardous site, or water-infeasible parcel
- Ecological baselines and outcome measures tailored to the actual ecosystem
- Safe water, sanitation, shelter, fire, access, and emergency planning
- Long-term stewardship covenant and anti-flipping design

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not tell anyone to occupy, build on, plant, or survey public land.
- Do not call degraded land ‘dead’ in technical settings; many disturbed sites retain ecological and cultural value.
- Do not promise off-grid living is legal or safe without permits and services.
- Do not promise a parcel, food forest, title, or zero-cost lifestyle.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Can I apply now? | No. This is a proposal. No parcel or application exists unless future law and an official program say so. |
| What counts as restoration? | The statute would need site-specific baselines and measurable soil, water, native-cover, biodiversity, fire, and productivity outcomes. |
| Why grant title? | The platform treats permanent ownership as the earned return for restoring public value, subject to safeguards. |
| What if the project fails? | Law must define safe exit, remediation, support, and reallocation without trapping a person in debt or leaving damage. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Never advertise or scout a specific parcel as available.
2. Host a design review with tribal governments, ecologists, public-land users, rural counties, water experts, fire officials, builders, disability advocates, and prospective homesteaders.
3. Use NRCS resources to explain soil and water principles without claiming they are a complete homestead standard.
4. Ask audiences to identify exclusions before discussing acreage.
