# Lunar Far Side Reserve Policy Action Kit

*Protect radio silence while enabling treaty-consistent science and limited use.*

Explain the Fill the Cosmos with Earthlife proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The campaign proposes an international reserve with nested protection zones on the Moon’s far side to preserve exceptional radio quiet for science while allowing controlled corridors and treaty-consistent use without a sovereignty claim.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

Earth’s radio emissions and future lunar transmitters can contaminate very-low-frequency observations of the cosmic dark ages. Once large-scale infrastructure spreads, the far side’s natural radio shield may be difficult to restore.

1. Use international scientific modeling to define interference limits and nested zones, with a carefully justified south-polar exception rather than campaign-drawn borders.
2. Create a broad low-impact outer area, a central science park for radio-dependent research, and the most restrictive core where radio silence is essential.
3. Coordinate lunar spectrum, orbital relays, navigation, emergency communication, dust, light, vibration, landing, mining, and infrastructure standards.
4. Establish secure, renewable rights to use built facilities and occupied sites without claiming national sovereignty or ownership of the Moon.
5. Create monitoring, registration, inspection, dispute resolution, liability, emergency, and technology-update rules through international agreement.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Outer Space Treaty compliance and no national appropriation
- Science-defined interference thresholds and transparent allocation
- Emergency safety and communication exceptions
- No medical claim that radio-free residence treats illness
- Rights of all countries and peaceful users considered through international negotiation

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say the United States can unilaterally create or own a lunar reserve.
- Do not map permanent boundaries as settled.
- Do not claim the far side is perfectly radio silent or the only scientifically useful location.
- Do not make health claims about a radio-free core.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why the far side? | The Moon can shield low-frequency instruments from much of Earth’s radio noise, enabling observations difficult from Earth or near-Earth space. |
| Does this claim lunar territory? | No. The platform expressly rejects sovereignty; the legal framework must comply with the Outer Space Treaty. |
| Can people still visit? | The concept allows low-impact corridors and science access under interference and safety limits. |
| Why act early? | Radio contamination and infrastructure placement can create path dependence before international protection exists. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Share NASA’s FARSIDE concept as evidence of scientific interest, not as endorsement of the campaign’s legal framework.
2. Host a discussion with radio astronomers, space-law scholars, commercial operators, international participants, disability/health experts, and planetary-protection voices.
3. Emphasize treaty consistency and unresolved boundaries.
4. Do not suggest the campaign can allocate lunar sites.
