# Citizens Share Money Creation Program Action Kit

*Route a minority share of newly created money through citizens who fund builders and public benefit.*

Explain the Complete the Revolution proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The program would direct 20 percent of newly created central-bank money into a rule-governed Citizens Share that allocates half to nonprofit grants and half to repayable loans and investments, while the Federal Reserve retains control of the total money supply.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

The platform argues that when monetary expansion enters primarily through securities markets and major financial intermediaries, those closest to the entry point receive the earliest opportunity while small builders and communities receive little direct allocation power.

1. Keep the Federal Reserve responsible for the total amount of money created and its monetary-policy objectives.
2. Maintain zero-premium Citizen Bonds as a fixed share of Federal Reserve holdings while ordinary open-market operations continue with the balance.
3. Route 20 percent of newly created money to a citizen allocation system governed by uniform public rules and checks and balances.
4. Split each cycle between nonprofit grants and repayable loans/investments.
5. Use transparent performance measures so allocators whose choices create verified value receive proportionally more responsibility in later cycles.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- The Federal Reserve retains control of aggregate money creation
- Rules against conflicts, self-dealing, fraud, coordination, discrimination, and capture
- Independent inflation, distributional, fiscal, and financial-stability modeling before launch
- Auditable decisions, privacy protection, appeals, randomization/rotation, and portfolio limits
- Pilot scale, automatic correction, and shutdown thresholds

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not say the Fed simply hands money to banks; describe open-market operations accurately.
- Do not claim primary dealers alone decide where every new dollar goes.
- Do not promise free personal cash; allocations fund nonprofits and repayable ventures under the proposal.
- Do not claim the 20 percent share is proven noninflationary before modeling and legislation.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Does this increase the amount of money created? | The platform says no: the Fed still determines the total; the proposal changes the first destination of a minority share. |
| Why 20 percent? | That is the platform’s proposed share. It would need independent macroeconomic scoring and pilot rules. |
| Who gets to allocate? | Citizens operating under published checks, balances, uniform rules, conflict controls, and performance review; the detailed selection system remains to be legislated. |
| Is this fiscal spending? | The proposal uses a new monetary-policy asset and allocation channel. Its legal/accounting treatment would require careful congressional and Federal Reserve analysis. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Use Federal Reserve and New York Fed pages to explain the current baseline before presenting the proposal.
2. Host a red-team discussion with monetary economists, community lenders, nonprofit leaders, small founders, and governance experts.
3. Ask what anti-capture, privacy, inflation, and performance tests a pilot must pass.
4. Avoid slogans that turn a technical institutional proposal into a guaranteed personal payment.
