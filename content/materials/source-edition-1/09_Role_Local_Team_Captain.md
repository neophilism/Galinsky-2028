# Local Team Captain Playbook

*Coordinate a small volunteer team without creating a shadow campaign.*

Maintain a reliable local rhythm, distribute approved work, protect legal boundaries, and make the team resilient when nobody is in charge full-time.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Weekly action cycle and two-person continuity
- Central truth with decentralized execution
- No contracts, unauthorized spending, personal databases, or invented policy

> **Your 60-minute start:** Recruit one co-captain, choose one green-lane weekly action, and schedule a twenty-minute check-in using the standard agenda.

## Set up the action

1. Pair every captain with a backup who can access public materials but not personal credentials.
2. Choose one weekly objective and one number that shows completion.
3. Assign work by role packet, not by improvised title.
4. Begin each meeting with safety, consent, data, and legal stop conditions.
5. Use official forms for signups and reporting; keep no duplicate roster.
6. Publish no local statement or event as campaign-official unless the campaign system authorizes it.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** Our team’s job is to make lawful action easy. We use the campaign’s published materials, choose small measurable tasks, and stop rather than guess when money, data, automation, ballots, or permits are involved.

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** This week’s objective is [one outcome]. Each person owns one action and reports anonymous totals. Our next check-in asks what happened, what we learned, and what we change.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| A volunteer goes off-message | Correct the specific claim, share the source, and move them to nonpublic work if the pattern continues. |
| Nobody completes tasks | Cut task size in half and assign named owners with dates. |
| A conflict forms | Address behavior and rules, not factional loyalty; remove unsafe conduct from activity. |
| A promising tactic needs money or legal review | Document it and pause; do not fund it locally. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Opening a local bank account or collecting dues
- Entering contracts, leases, insurance arrangements, or vendor relationships
- Creating an independent voter/supporter database
- Representing the team as the campaign committee or issuing official statements

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
