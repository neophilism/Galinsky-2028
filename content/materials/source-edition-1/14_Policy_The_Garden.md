# The Garden Policy Action Kit

*Self-government and an economic inheritance for American descendants of slavery.*

Explain the Justice for All proposal accurately, include its safeguards and limits, answer predictable questions, and offer lawful volunteer actions.

> **For:** Issue ambassadors, personal-network organizers, digital ambassadors, hosts, callers, canvassers, and policy reviewers

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## The short version

> **One-sentence explanation:** The campaign proposes a voluntary process through which eligible American descendants of slavery could form self-governing republican tribal nations connected to documented historic African nations and negotiate carefully limited economic compacts.

> **Always say:** This is a campaign proposal, not a current program or personal entitlement. Legislation, public rulemaking, implementation safeguards, and often state or international participation would be required.

## The problem and the mechanism

The platform argues that slavery destroyed not only liberty, labor, family continuity, and inheritance, but also citizenship in identifiable African nations. It treats that unrepaired loss of nationhood and self-government as a specific public debt.

1. Fund archival work to establish a documented roster of historic nations involved in the forced migration of enslaved people.
2. Use American Descendants of Slavery ancestry to establish participation, while allowing eligible descendants—not government—to choose which voluntary nation to join during an open founding period.
3. Let each nation write a constitution, elect leaders, and decide future citizenship under republican rules; a coordinating assembly would facilitate but not bind nations.
4. Allow participating states to offer a narrowly defined commercial opportunity only where that activity is prohibited to everyone in the district and no existing worker, licensee, customer, or market participant would be displaced.
5. Advance limited formation costs through a revolving Founding Fund repaid by successful enterprises under fixed terms.

## Safeguards that belong in the pitch

- Voluntary participation and self-selection among documented historic nations
- No government racial assignment and no displacement of an existing lawful livelihood
- State safety, labor, consumer, environmental, and anti-corruption law remains
- New entities would not automatically be federally recognized Indian tribes and must not diminish the sovereignty, lands, treaties, rights, or political status of Native nations
- Congressional and participating-state legislation, constitutional analysis, archival standards, and public rulemaking would be required

## What not to claim

> **Accuracy boundary:** A volunteer earns trust by naming limits before an opponent has to.

- Do not call the proposed nations existing federally recognized tribes.
- Do not claim ancestry records can identify every person or nation with certainty.
- Do not present a monopoly as a grant over a currently lawful competing business.
- Do not speak for ADOS communities, African nations, or Native nations; invite their own voices.

## Questions people will ask

| Question | Grounded answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is this based on race? | The platform defines initial eligibility through descent from people enslaved in the United States and proposes voluntary nation choice. The constitutional design, evidentiary rules, and relationship to existing tribal law would require rigorous public and legal review. |
| Does this take from someone else? | The published safeguard bars an offered trade where anyone already holds or competes for a job, license, contract, customer, queue position, or lawful market in the district. |
| Is this the same as Native tribal recognition? | No. The proposal uses a new framework. It must expressly protect the distinct sovereignty and treaty rights of federally recognized tribes. |
| Who decides the historic nation? | Eligible descendants would choose during a founding period from a documented roster; government would not assign individual ancestry. |

## A two-minute conversation

1. Ask what the person wants solved before naming the policy.
2. State the problem in one sentence without exaggerating it.
3. Give the one-sentence explanation and the two most relevant safeguards.
4. Name the main dependency or unresolved implementation question.
5. Ask: ‘What would you need to see tested before this became law?’
6. Offer the official platform and primary sources; do not demand agreement.

> **Close:** The value of this proposal is that it gives us something specific to test. The full campaign text is at galinsky2028.us, and I’d be interested in the strongest question you think the legislation must answer.

## Actions volunteers can take

1. Read the platform plank before discussing its sensitive historical and legal claims.
2. Host a listening session centered on questions from ADOS historians, genealogists, constitutional scholars, African-diaspora institutions, and Native sovereignty experts.
3. Share the one-sentence explanation with the full official text and invite scrutiny of the non-displacement and sovereignty safeguards.
4. Collect questions anonymously through the official volunteer form; do not collect ancestry documents.
