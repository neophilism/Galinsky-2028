# Skills & Operations Volunteer Playbook

*Contribute design, research, writing, translation, accessibility, logistics, or technical skill safely.*

Convert professional and practical skills into bounded campaign deliverables without exposing data, violating rights, or deploying unauthorized work.

> **For:** A self-directed campaign volunteer; captains may use this for peer onboarding

> **Status:** Campaign proposal and volunteer guidance. Verify changing law and campaign instructions before regulated activity.

## Required foundation

> **Read first:** Complete the Universal Volunteer Core before using this playbook. Its truth, privacy, consent, spending, safety, and automation rules apply here in full.

## What success looks like

- Clear briefs, sources, licenses, and review status
- No production deployment or data access by default
- Accessible, reproducible handoffs another volunteer can verify

> **Your 60-minute start:** Choose one public-source task, write a five-line brief, create a small draft, and submit it through the current official volunteer channel without publishing it as campaign-official.

## Set up the action

1. Define the audience, output, deadline, approved sources, non-goals, and reviewer test.
2. Work only from public material unless an official system grants least-privilege access.
3. Use assets you created, the campaign supplied, or a documented license permits.
4. Keep a source list and distinguish quotation, paraphrase, inference, and proposal.
5. Check plain language, color contrast, keyboard access, captions, alt text, and mobile readability as relevant.
6. Deliver editable source files plus a final export; label drafts clearly and remove private metadata.

## Words you can use

Opening script

> **Say this in your own voice:** I can contribute [skill]. I propose a bounded deliverable: [output] for [audience], based only on [public sources], with [verification method]. I will not publish or deploy it without authorization.

Follow-up script

> **Offer one next step:** The handoff includes source files, fonts/licenses, source links, accessibility notes, assumptions, known limitations, and a pass/fail checklist.

Scripts are guardrails, not a performance. Keep required identification and legal language exact; otherwise sound like yourself.

## Run the action

1. Review the legal, safety, consent, and privacy checks before the first contact.
2. Begin with the smallest useful batch so errors are cheap to correct.
3. Ask permission and give the other person an easy exit.
4. Use only current official links, forms, lists, scripts, and unaltered materials.
5. Record only the requested result; honor opt-outs immediately.
6. Stop on any red-lane condition.
7. Debrief in three lines and choose the next action.

- [ ] Identity was clear.
- [ ] Consent was respected.
- [ ] Claims stayed within published material.
- [ ] No private list or unauthorized spending was created.
- [ ] The next volunteer can understand the handoff.

## When something goes wrong

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| No one can review quickly | Keep it a clearly labeled draft and work on reversible/public-source tasks. |
| You receive private data unexpectedly | Do not copy it; restrict access and report through the official channel. |
| A source is paywalled or cannot be verified | Replace it with a primary public source or remove the claim. |
| A translation has no second reviewer | Label it unofficial and do not publish as authoritative. |

## Stop conditions

> **Do not proceed:** Stop, preserve safety and evidence where appropriate, and switch to a green-lane action if any condition below applies.

- Deploying code, changing DNS, publishing on official accounts, or granting access
- Scraping, buying, enriching, or matching personal data
- Uploading campaign/private information into third-party tools without authorization
- Using unlicensed creative work or presenting a draft as approved

## Quality scorecard

| Measure | Good | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Every factual claim is in current campaign material or a dated primary source. | A number, quote, status, or promise cannot be verified. |
| Consent | People can decline easily; opt-outs are immediate. | Repeated contact, pressure, or hidden recording. |
| Privacy | Official forms handle personal information; local notes are anonymous. | Names, contact data, screenshots, or exports on personal devices. |
| Completion | One action, one owner, one deadline, one anonymous result. | Vague activity without a finish line or handoff. |
